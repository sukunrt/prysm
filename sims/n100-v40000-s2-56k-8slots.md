# 100 nodes, 40,000 validators, two subnets, 56 KiB payloads

2026-10-06. Both simulations completed on ethp2p, launched in parallel:
Prysm 1456.07 wall seconds; eth-slot-sim 539.29.
All 100 nodes subscribed to both FFG subnets and both simulators published exactly
2,500 votes per committee per slot. The final execution payloads were close to
the target, but **the run did not maintain complete connectivity in Prysm** and
its final blob count differed from eth-slot-sim.

### Connectivity and peer scoring

Every Prysm node had all 99 CL and 99 EL peers before slot 1. At the final
snapshot, 35 nodes had fewer than 99 CL peers (range 81–99), and five had fewer
than 99 EL peers (range 97–99). eth-slot-sim passed all 900 connectivity audits.

Prysm explicitly logged **32 bad-peer disconnections**, all with the reason
`gossip score below threshold` and threshold −100. These map exactly to the
32 CL links missing at the final snapshot. Seven home nodes initiated them:
node4 (18), node6 (3), node7 (2), node10 (1), node25 (1), node92 (5), node93 (2).
They occurred from slot 7 + 6.79s through slot 8 + 3.00s. The three missing EL
links were 7–14, 7–93 and 44–76; their cause has not been established.

This is direct evidence that peer scoring changed connectivity. It does not
establish that those disconnections caused all missing records or the latency
gap: missing FFG records already appeared in slot 3, before these disconnections,
and final-slot missing records occurred at nodes16/19, which retained 99 peers.
The configured complete graph was achieved at startup but was not maintained
by Prysm's unmodified scoring/disconnection behavior.

A [Prysm rerun with staggered FFG publication](n100-v40000-s2-56k-8slots-spread.md)
uses this experiment as its baseline and adds only `--decoupled-ffg-vote-spread`.

The driver stopped at its failed connectivity assertion. The remaining receipt,
column, across-slot and two-subnet audits were then run explicitly; all those
scripts completed. The failed connectivity result is retained, not overridden.

The 40,000 validators are a total population, maintaining the previous skewed

keys are reused. Eight staggered validator groups each contain 5,000 validators;
each group's two subnet committees contain exactly 2,500. In eth-slot-sim a
seeded shuffle constructs the balanced round/subnet assignment before schedule
generation. The validator-segregation option stays enabled. No Prysm FFG delay
or spread flag is used, and configured FFG jitter is zero.

The one-subnet setting from earlier experiments has changed only for FFG.
Data-column assignments remain 128 column subnets, eight per home and all 128
per super. All nodes being connected does not imply all peers belong to each
eight-peer gossip mesh. Existing scoring, transport and PTC sending policies
are retained: Prysm can send PTC when ready, while eth-slot-sim sends at 6s.

## Execution and blob workload

Spamoor injects one EOA transaction per slot with 56,320 bytes (55 KiB) of random
calldata, four maximum pending transactions and 32 wallets. Encoding and block
access-list overhead bring the execution payload near the 56 KiB target. Its
transaction gas allowance is 4,000,000 to accommodate the calldata; the block
gas limit stays at **200,000,000**. eth-slot-sim's payload is exactly 57,344 bytes.

The user confirmed restoring the earlier blob workload: two blob transactions
per slot, three blobs each, with at most eight pending.
The target is six blobs per slot; Prysm's actual included counts can vary and
will be reported. eth-slot-sim models six blobs. Blob bytes are separate from
the execution payload size target. Both existing binary sets are reused; the
run-scoped no-blob-capable eth-slot-sim binary has `no_blob_data=false` here.
Its normal column-publication path and original connectivity audit stay enabled.

The actual included workload varied. Slots 1–2 were empty during startup;
slot 4 missed the large EOA transaction and slot 5 included two. Slots 3–8
averaged 57,831 bytes (+0.85% from target), but this average hides the variation:

Slot 3 passed the first end-to-end workload check: two EL transactions, three

block hashes. Its gas use was 3,640,480 against the 200,000,000 block limit.

## Validation and reports

Before launch the driver checks total validators, two subnet memberships for
all eth-slot-sim nodes, exactly `[2500, 2500]` voters in all eight rounds, timing
flags, gas configuration, binary hashes, and the shared network mapping.
Postprocessing verifies actual Prysm subscription logs and publication counts
per committee, and audits real peer identities before/after the Prysm run and
at every eth-slot-sim slot boundary.

The comparison reports final-slot average, median, p95 and p99, with separate
home/super receiver results. Across-slot FFG reports also break out each subnet
and receiver class. Missing receipts are reported explicitly; quantiles use
observed receipts. Aggregate results retain the unconditional ignored-message
ledger and separate accepted/ignored outcomes.
